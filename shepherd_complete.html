CHAMBER 1 · THE CORE INSIGHT

The Architecture Already Exists

From asteroid capture to cislunar logistics — one recognition

The Hybrid Tethered Centrifugal Accelerator established in SOP-003 v6.6 was designed to deliver asteroids to lunar transfer orbits. The same tether, rotation, and release geometry that moves a 1,000-tonne asteroid moves anything else. The architecture does not need to be rebuilt for logistics — it needs to be recognized as logistics.

A team of Shepherds stationed at a fixed orbital position is a relay node. Two teams at different positions form a lane. Multiple lanes form a network. The solar system becomes navigable through coordinated rotational mechanics — not continuous propulsion. Shepherds are called Shepherds because that is what they do. They guide.

SpinLaunch has demonstrated the same rotational principle from the ground — a vacuum centrifuge that flings payloads at up to 8,000 kph using electricity rather than rocket fuel. Ten successful suborbital tests. NASA partnership confirmed. The gap SpinLaunch cannot close internally: nothing in orbit to catch what it launches. The Shepherd closes that gap.

The Shepherd does not move cargo. The Shepherd moves energy into cargo — and guides it. The cargo travels the rest of the way carrying nothing but a location broadcaster and a capture collar. The architecture is the same at every scale: asteroid, structural component, supply package, Shepherd in transit. The invariant holds across all of them.

PART II · HOW IT OPERATES
CHAMBER 3 · THE RELAY NODE

Capture, Guide, Release

How a team of Shepherds becomes a station

Each relay node consists of a team of 4–8 Shepherds in fixed orbital formation, maintaining tether web geometry continuously. Incoming cargo — whether asteroid material, manufactured components, or a Shepherd in transit — is captured by the tether web, absorbed into the rotation, and re-slung toward the next node at the required velocity.

The Shepherd team adjusts three variables per cargo event: release velocity matched to payload tolerance, release timing matched to destination orbital geometry, and release angle matched to required trajectory. The payload's constraints are inputs. The architecture absorbs them.

CAPTURE

Incoming cargo intersects tether web. Rotational energy absorbed gradually. No impact loading. Plasma torque compensates for mass asymmetry. Broadcaster tracks position throughout approach.

GUIDE

Cargo held in rotation cycle. Node adjusts spin rate and release parameters. Multiple pre-release correction cycles verify arc before committing. Window computed against destination orbital position.

RELEASE

Tether released at verified moment. Cargo departs on confirmed ballistic arc. Node returns to neutral rotation and restores spin via plasma torque. No fuel consumed by payload in transit.

The node is not a static platform. It is a machine that continuously reads cargo, reads orbital geometry, computes the arc, and releases with precision. The Shepherd guides because guiding is what it was built to do.
CHAMBER 4 · ORBITAL INVENTORY

Cargo That Waits Is Not Cargo That's Lost

Broadcaster-guided parking orbit as buffer between sling and delivery

Not all cargo needs immediate delivery. Bulk material, structural components, and supply packages can be slung into a parking orbit — lunar low orbit, L1, L2, or near-rectilinear halo orbit — and held until the recipient is ready to collect or the destination node opens a capture window. This decouples the sling event from the delivery event entirely.

Each cargo item carries a passive location broadcaster. Minimal mass — under 200 g. Minimal power draw — under 100 mW. Battery life: 10 years. The Shepherd reads every broadcaster in the inventory orbit continuously. When an item drifts, the Shepherd guides it back with a small delta-v correction. When the capture window opens, the Shepherd guides the item to the tether web.

VERIFIED · DORKED Near-rectilinear halo orbits (NRHO) around Earth-Moon L2 have been characterized for long-duration parking. NASA Gateway is planned for this orbit. Station-keeping cost for active spacecraft: 2–5 m/s per year. For passive cargo with Shepherd guidance corrections — periodic small delta-v per item. Cost is low relative to cargo value. The same argument that justifies warehouse management justifies orbital inventory management.

The network is never rushed. The node slings when the geometry is right. Cargo accumulates in orbital inventory. Recipients draw on demand. The celestial mechanics that seemed like a scheduling constraint become the storage architecture.

CHAMBER 5 · TRAJECTORY AND ABORT

The Node Holds Responsibility

Arc verification before release · Shepherd escort for sensitive cargo

A ballistic arc over 384,000 km accumulates perturbations — lunar gravity, solar radiation pressure, Earth oblateness. The node adjusts release parameters during the hold window before cargo departs. Multiple small correction cycles allow the node to verify the arc before committing. The cargo departs on a trajectory the node has confirmed.

For sensitive cargo: a Shepherd is paired with the payload for the full transit. It carries correction and abort capability, travels with the cargo, maintains relative position, corrects any arc deviation, and returns to the originating node after delivery. The cargo carries no propulsion. The Shepherd carries everything active — and the Shepherd comes back.

The node holds arc responsibility. The paired Shepherd holds abort capability. The cargo holds neither. Accountability assigned to recoverable, reusable nodes — not to the payload that cannot recover itself.

CHAMBER 7 · SHEPHERD SELF-TRANSIT

The Network Moves Itself

Fleet redistribution as a scheduling problem, not a propulsion problem

A Shepherd requiring repositioning does not burn its own fuel for transit. It enters the tether web of the nearest relay node and gets slung toward its destination. The network that moves cargo also moves the Shepherds that maintain the network.

A Shepherd at Node A reaches Node C via Node B, routed through normal cargo operations. No dedicated repositioning burns. No fuel budget for fleet management beyond what node operations already consume. The network is self-distributing as a natural consequence of the architecture applied to its own components.

The infrastructure does not require a separate logistics chain for its own mobility. The relay that moves cargo is the relay that moves the Shepherds. The geometry is the same at every scale.
