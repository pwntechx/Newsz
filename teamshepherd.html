ABSTRACT

Three architectures were evaluated for delivering whole M-class asteroids to lunar orbit using a fleet of ten Shepherd spacecraft. The magnetic storage ring (v6.3) failed: passive diamagnetic confinement was shown to be 9 orders of magnitude short of the required centripetal force. The pure tethered centrifugal sling (v6.5) succeeded physically but imposed a severe mass penalty: 494 tonnes of tether at current material strengths for a 3,700-tonne asteroid. This audit (v6.6) evaluates a hybrid architecture combining mechanical tension for radial constraint with plasma torque for angular acceleration and active centering.

The hybrid does not reduce tether mass — the tethers must be sized for the full centripetal load in the plasma-off failure case. What it provides is control: active drift damping, load equalization, eccentricity suppression, and redundancy. The hybrid is the correct architecture not because it is lighter, but because it is more survivable.

For a 1,000-tonne asteroid at current material strengths, it is buildable today. For the original 3,700-tonne design case, it remains conditional on materials development to 30+ GPa macro-cable.

§ 01 — ARCHITECTURE GENEALOGY

The Evolution of the Solution

The problem is unchanged: deliver whole M-class asteroids to lunar orbit for in-situ refining. The Shepherd v3.0 baseline launches 1-tonne boulders at 2,400 tonnes per year per spacecraft. Whole-asteroid delivery requires accelerating masses of 1,000–100,000 tonnes to velocities of 300–500 m/s.

v6.3 — Magnetic Storage Ring. Ten Shepherds in a circle. Plasma columns pointing inward. Asteroid confined by diamagnetic repulsion and accelerated by repeated linear plasma impulses. Failed audit: passive diamagnetic force at 100-meter range ~10⁻⁶ N; required centripetal force 9.25 × 10⁹ N. Gap: 1.2 × 10⁹. Additionally, linear impulse efficiency η was identified as an unmeasured single-point failure. Architecture rejected.

v6.5 — Pure Tethered Centrifugal Sling. Ten Shepherds physically tethered to the asteroid. Tether web carries centripetal load in tension. Shepherds apply rotational torque via plasma columns. Architecture physically sound but used an aspirational 50 GPa tether strength — 16× above current macro-scale CNT cable at 3 GPa. At measured material strengths, tether web masses 494 tonnes for 3,700-tonne asteroid. Architecture accepted for sub-1,000-tonne targets; conditional on materials development for larger bodies.

v6.6 — Hybrid Tethered Centrifugal Accelerator (this audit). Combines mechanical tether constraint with plasma torque and active centering. Addresses the question: can the two subsystems complement each other to produce a more robust architecture than either alone?

§ 02 — HYBRID PRINCIPLE

Separation of Concerns

Each subsystem is assigned the function it performs without reliance on unverified physics:

Tether Web: Static Radial Constraint. Carries the time-averaged centripetal force F_c = mv²/r. Physics: σ = F/A, classical mechanics. Material: Zylon at 5.8 GPa or CNT macro-cable at 3 GPa — measured, commercially available strengths. The tethers are sized for the full centripetal load in the plasma-off failure case.

Plasma Columns: Angular Acceleration and Active Radial Correction. The Shepherds apply rotational torque via their plasma columns — the mode for which the Shepherd v3.0 document derived 92% theoretical coupling efficiency. No linear impulse efficiency η is required. During each 125 MJ capacitor discharge, the plasma pulse can be shaped to include a brief radial component, providing an inward correction kick synchronized with the asteroid's rotation.

Redundancy. If plasma torque drops offline, the tethers hold the asteroid at its current velocity — no catastrophic release. If a tether fails, plasma correction kicks provide transient centering while the remaining tethers absorb the redistributed load. No single-point physics failure kills the mission.

The plasma column is not a load-bearing tether. It is a control system. The tether handles the Load (Classical Physics); the plasma handles the Drift (Dynamics).
§ 03 — WHAT THE PLASMA ACTUALLY PROVIDES

Control, Not Mass Savings

The v6.4 hybrid proposal claimed plasma could provide 10–50% of the centripetal force, proportionally reducing tether mass. This claim was incorrect and is formally withdrawn.

Correct calculation: During a 125 MJ plasma pulse, the magnetic field at the asteroid surface reaches approximately 1 Tesla with gradient ∇B ≈ 10 T/m over a pulse duration of approximately 1 millisecond. For a diamagnetic nickel-iron asteroid of volume 400 m³ and magnetic susceptibility χ ≈ −10⁻⁵:

F_peak = (χV/μ₀) × B × ∇B ≈ (10⁻⁵ × 400 / 4π×10⁻⁷) × 1 × 10 ≈ 3.2 × 10⁴ N

Pulses per day: 69 (10 Shepherds × 24 hours / 3.47 hours per charge). Total pulse duration per day: 69 × 0.001 s = 0.069 seconds. Time-averaged radial force: 3.2 × 10⁴ × 0.069 / 86,400 ≈ 0.026 N.

Required centripetal force for 3,700 tonnes at 500 m/s, 100-meter radius: 9.25 × 10⁹ N.

The plasma provides approximately 0.0000000003% of the mean centripetal force. This does not reduce tether cross-section. The tethers must carry the full centripetal load because they must survive the plasma-off case.

What the plasma actually provides:

· Drift damping. Without active correction, solar radiation pressure, outgassing, and tether creep cause the asteroid to drift radially. Over 54 days of spin-up, uncorrected drift accumulates to eccentricity that produces cyclic tether loading and potential fatigue failure. Sixty-nine correction kicks per day prevent this accumulation.

· Load equalization. Pulse timing can be biased to apply slightly more radial force on the side where tethers are slack, actively balancing load distribution across the web.

· Eccentricity suppression. Active centering keeps the path circular, maintaining constant tether tension and preventing cyclic loading.

· Emergency response. If a tether fails, the plasma can apply an inward impulse within the next pulse cycle, reducing the peak load on the remaining tethers and buying time for controlled de-spin.

CORRECTION FROM v6.4 Claim: "Plasma provides 10–50% of centripetal force." False. Provides <0.0000001% mean. Claim: "Hybrid halves tether mass." False. Hybrid adds control; does not reduce cross-sectional requirement.
§ 04 — TETHER WEB: MASS BUDGET

Unchanged by the Hybrid

Because the tethers must carry the full centripetal load in the failure case, the mass budget from v6.5 is unchanged. The hybrid adds plasma control authority over the same tether mass.

For a 1,000-tonne asteroid at 500 m/s, 100-meter radius:

F_c = (1.0 × 10⁶)(500)² / 100 = 2.5 × 10⁹ N

At 5.8 GPa (Zylon, commercially available): Cross-section 0.431 m² total. 100 tethers at 4.31 × 10⁻³ m² per tether, diameter 7.4 cm each. Tether web mass: 67 tonnes.

For a 3,700-tonne asteroid at 500 m/s, 100-meter radius:

F_c = 9.25 × 10⁹ N

At 5.8 GPa: Cross-section 1.59 m², mass 248 tonnes.
At 3.0 GPa (CNT macro-cable, current): Cross-section 3.08 m², mass 494 tonnes.
At 30 GPa (CNT development target): Cross-section 0.308 m², mass 49 tonnes.

The tether mass is determined by material strength and asteroid mass. The hybrid adds no mass reduction. It adds control functions that use the Shepherd's existing plasma systems — no additional hardware beyond what the Shepherds already carry.

§ 05 — ACCELERATION: ROTATIONAL TORQUE

The Shepherd's Designed Function

Spin-up uses the Shepherd's rotational torque mode. The 92% theoretical coupling efficiency from Shepherd v3.0 applies. No η required.

Energy required: E = ½mv²
For 1,000 tonnes at 500 m/s: 1.25 × 10¹¹ J
For 3,700 tonnes at 500 m/s: 4.625 × 10¹¹ J

Fleet energy delivery rate: 10 Shepherds × 125 MJ / 3.47 hours = 360 MJ/hour

Spin-up time, 1,000 tonnes: 1.25 × 10¹¹ / 360 × 10⁶ = 347 hours = 14.5 days
Spin-up time, 3,700 tonnes: 4.625 × 10¹¹ / 360 × 10⁶ = 1,285 hours = 53.5 days

The 92% figure is walled pending 100-meter vacuum chamber demonstration. If effective torque is lower, spin-up time scales proportionally. At 50% effective torque, 1,000 tonnes requires 29 days; 3,700 tonnes requires 107 days. The architecture remains viable within these bounds.

§ 06 — OPERATIONAL SEQUENCE

Five Phases to Lunar Transfer

PHASE 1 — RENDEZVOUS AND DE-SPIN (days to weeks)

Ten Shepherds approach the asteroid. Plasma columns engage in torque mode to brake natural rotation to near-zero. This is the Shepherd baseline "de-spin for mining" mode.

PHASE 2 — SURFACE SURVEY AND ANCHOR INSTALLATION (days)

High-resolution surface mapping identifies competent anchor points in monolithic nickel-iron. Each Shepherd deploys a drilling rig to install expansion bolts or resin-anchored pins at 1–5 meter depth. Each anchor proof-tested to 150% of nominal load before spin-up. Ten anchor points per Shepherd, one hundred total.

PHASE 3 — TETHER DEPLOYMENT AND TENSIONING (hours to days)

Each Shepherd deploys its ten tether strands sequentially under controlled tension to prevent tangling. Free ends guided to anchor points and locked. All one hundred tethers tensioned to equal load via motorized winches with closed-loop load cell feedback. Target tolerance: ±1% load variation.

PHASE 4 — SPIN-UP WITH ACTIVE CENTERING (14.5 days for 1,000 t; 53.5 days for 3,700 t)

Plasma columns engage in torque mode. Tangential force produces angular acceleration. Radial pulse component provides centering correction. SQUID magnetometer arrays on each Shepherd monitor asteroid position. If radial drift exceeds threshold, pulse shaping biases inward on the next discharge. Tether tension monitored continuously. If any tether exceeds 90% rated load, spin-up pauses for load redistribution.

PHASE 5 — RELEASE (milliseconds)

At target tangential velocity, all one hundred tether anchors release simultaneously via explosive bolts or magnetic disconnects. Release signal: pre-programmed classical RF. Timing requirement: <100 ms jitter across all points. Asteroid exits tangentially on lunar transfer trajectory. Shepherds retract tethers and reconfigure for next target.

§ 07 — FAILURE MODES

Introduced by Integration

Combining plasma columns with a tether web introduces failure modes not present in either pure architecture:

F-HYBRID-01 — Plasma Exposure Degradation. During each 125 MJ discharge, the plasma column's outer edge may intersect tether strands. At 3 eV argon plasma temperature, sputtering yield on carbon-based tether material is low but cumulative. Over 1,000 pulses, even 10⁻⁴ atoms/ion sputtering may erode tether surface. UV and thermal pulse may cause polymer matrix degradation in composite tethers. Action: Materials exposure testing required.

F-HYBRID-02 — Pulse Shaping Feasibility. The Shepherd's plasma column is designed for pure torque. Adding a radial component requires modifying the magnetic nozzle geometry or quadrupole field during the pulse. This may destabilize plasma confinement, reducing the 92% coupling efficiency. If pulse shaping proves infeasible, the hybrid reverts to pure tether sling behavior — no radial correction, but torque still functional.

F-HYBRID-03 — Tether Shadowing of Plasma Column. One hundred tethers at 100-meter radius with 628-meter circumference produce average tether spacing of approximately 6 meters. Each plasma column expands conically from 0.5 meters diameter at the Shepherd toward the asteroid. If significant shadowing occurs, plasma energy is absorbed by tethers rather than coupled to the asteroid. Action: 3D geometric modeling and routing optimization required.

F-HYBRID-04 — Control Loop Stability. The feedback loop — SQUID detects radial drift, command adjusts pulse bias, plasma corrects — introduces latency. Plasma pulses occur at 69 per day. Between pulses, the asteroid drifts freely. The control system must maintain eccentricity below 1% with this low-frequency correction. Action: Control theory analysis and 6-DOF simulation required.

F-HYBRID-05 — Anchor Cyclic Loading. Active centering reduces mean eccentricity but does not eliminate cyclic loading entirely. Each anchor point experiences 69 load cycles per day from pulse-induced transients. Over 1,000+ cycles per mission, fatigue may reduce anchor holding capacity. Action: Cyclic loading fatigue testing on monolithic anchors required.

§ 12 — CONCLUSION

The Lunar Supply Chain

The hybrid tethered centrifugal accelerator is the correct architecture for whole-asteroid delivery. It assigns each subsystem the function it performs without unverified physics: tethers carry the centripetal load in tension (classical mechanics, measured material strengths), plasma columns apply rotational torque (Shepherd baseline, 92% theoretical coupling) and active radial correction (pulsed centering, 69 kicks per day).

The hybrid does not reduce tether mass versus the pure sling. The tethers must be sized for the full centripetal load in the plasma-off failure case. The plasma provides approximately 0.0000000003% of the mean centripetal force — negligible for load sharing, valuable for drift damping, load equalization, eccentricity suppression, and emergency response.

For a 1,000-tonne M-class asteroid at current material strengths (5.8 GPa Zylon), the hybrid is buildable today. Tether mass 67 tonnes, spin-up time 14.5 days, release to lunar transfer. Total system mass (30 tonnes Shepherds + 67 tonnes tethers) is approximately 10% of delivered payload mass.

For a 3,700-tonne asteroid, the hybrid is conditional on materials development. At current 3 GPa CNT macro-cable, tether mass is 494 tonnes. At 30 GPa development target, tether mass is 49 tonnes. The architecture is defined and the development path is specified. The gap is measurable and has clear milestones.

No unverified physics parameters gate the mission. No η. No passive magnetic confinement. No RT/ER=EPR coordination requirement. Classical mechanics throughout. The open nodes are engineering: plasma exposure tolerance, pulse shaping feasibility, tether routing geometry, control loop stability, anchor fatigue. All are testable with current laboratory capabilities.

The hybrid is not lighter than the pure sling. It is more survivable. For the same tether mass, it adds active control that prevents the failure modes most likely to kill a purely passive system during 14–54 days of continuous spin-up. That is worth the integration complexity.

Build the 1,000-tonne demonstrator. Test plasma exposure on tethers. Model pulse shaping. Characterize anchor fatigue. Scale when materials allow.
