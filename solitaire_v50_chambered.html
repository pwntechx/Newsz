REGULATORY CORRECTION — NPS PRINCIPLES, NOT OST ARTICLE IX
The relevant regulatory instrument for nuclear power in space is UN Resolution 47/68 (Principles Relevant to the Use of Nuclear Power Sources in Outer Space, 1992) — not OST Article IX, which covers harmful contamination. The NPS Principles require sufficient altitude for orbital decay to allow radioactive decay before reentry, and safety reviews before launch. The inert launch strategy is designed to satisfy NPS Principles: no fission products at launch, criticality only at altitude where decay orbit is safe. This does not eliminate regulatory risk — it reduces it to a manageable path.
CHASM, NOT WALL — NAMED HONESTLY
No crewed NTR has ever flown. No commercial license exists for nuclear reactor operation in space. No regulatory framework for crewed NTR exists as of Year 47. The inert launch strategy reduces the regulatory burden but does not eliminate it. PATH A viability depends on crossing this prerequisite. PATH B (Fleet Protocol, ~7,500T) exists as a separate architecture if PATH A is not achievable within the mission timeline. The choice between PATH A and PATH B is political, not engineering.
VERDICT: WALLED. The inert launch path is defined. The regulatory framework does not yet exist. PATH B is the alternative architecture.
