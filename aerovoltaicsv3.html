Passive VLF Receiver (Atmospheric Sensing)

The tether is a 14 km vertical conductor. At VLF frequencies (3–30 kHz), the tether acts as an efficient monopole antenna. A passive receiver at the ground anchor captures:

  • Lightning sferics — global lightning mapping, storm tracking, atmospheric electricity research
  • Ionospheric disturbances — solar flares, geomagnetic storms, sudden ionospheric disturbances (SIDs)
  • Seismic precursors — ionospheric perturbations associated with large earthquakes (demonstrated in literature)

No transmission. No active signal. The platform listens. The data is science.

VLF Relay Transceiver (Emergency Communication)

VLF signals propagate via Earth-ionosphere waveguide, following the curvature of the Earth. They are not blocked by mountains or terrain. The 12 km altitude platform provides:

  • Line-of-sight VHF/UHF relay for maritime, aviation, and ground emergency communication
  • VLF fallback when satellites or HF are unavailable (disasters, infrastructure failure)
  • Global range — VLF signals circle the Earth

The platform is a tower. The tether is an antenna. The transceiver is low-power, software-defined, and operates on frequencies allocated for emergency and aeronautical use.

Platform Telemetry (Operations)

Telemetry is essential for remote operation. VLF provides a robust, independent channel not reliant on satellite networks.
