§ 00 · HOW TO READ THIS

Four Tags, Never Blended

Why a real number and a calculated one are never allowed to look the same

Chambers below are ordered by the physical sequence of the launch event — energy budget first, then launch mechanism, atmosphere, correction, and what's excluded — rather than by how the topics might have come up along the way. Each chamber carries one of four tags, and the tag is the whole point: it tells you exactly how much weight that specific claim can bear.

TAG DEFINITIONS
CITED
A real, named source states this number directly. The source is quoted in the chamber.
DERIVED
Computed from cited inputs using a named, standard formula — shown as formula plus result, not itself a citation.
OPEN
No source found. Stated plainly as unknown rather than estimated.
BOUNDARY
Real, solvable engineering that this auditor has chosen not to design, regardless of framing — a fixed decision, not a research gap.

If you only read one thing before the chambers: an OPEN or DERIVED tag isn't a failing grade. Most real engineering lives there — it just means nobody has published the specific number yet, or it has to be built up from parts that do exist. Take your time with the chambers that matter most to you; nothing here is trying to rush you to a conclusion, and the plain-language boxes are there so the physics stays legible even if the equations aren't your background.

§ 02 · LAUNCH MECHANISM
Launch Mechanism & Power Source
CITED
QUESTION
Can a circular magnetic launch ring reach multi-km/s, and is nuclear/grid power sourcing for the ring itself restricted?
EVIDENCE
Fiske et al. "Launch Ring" conceptual designRadial (centripetal) acceleration >10,000 g; launch speed >10 km/s, both reported achievable
Launch Ring reference case2,000 kg maglev sled to 9 km/s, ~1 hour spin-up, 20 MW (≈ one electric locomotive)
Circular Mass Accelerator patent filings (2018)Independently cite >5,000 g quasi-static loading for circular kinetic launchers
NASA MSFC Magnetic Launch AssistSub-scale linear maglev launch-assist track, built and tested; full-scale design: 600 mph in 9.5s
Mass Driver I (O'Neill & Kolm, 1977)First built coilgun/mass-driver prototype: 0.5 kg bucket to 36 m/s at 33 g
Sources: Fiske, Ricci, Ricci & Hull, "The Launch Ring — Circular EM Accelerators for Low Cost Orbital Launch," AIAA SPACE 2006 Conference, doi:10.2514/6.2006-7279 · US Patent filings referencing "Circular Mass Accelerator" (2018) · NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, Magnetic Launch Assist program documentation · G.K. O'Neill & H. Kolm, Mass Driver I, 1977
IN PLAIN TERMSPicture a maglev train track bent into a closed circle: a payload rides a magnetically-levitated sled around and around, gaining a little speed each lap from a linear synchronous motor — the same principle that pushes a maglev train forward, just looped. Once it's fast enough, it's released tangentially — like letting go of a rock on a string — into a straight exit rail that directs it out and away. The appeal of the circle is that you can spin up gradually and gently over many laps instead of needing enormous power in one short straight shot. The catch, and it's a real one, is that going in a circle at orbital speeds means enormous centripetal force trying to fling the payload outward — that's the "radial g" figure below, and it's the actual engineering bottleneck for this design, not the forward acceleration.
ASSESSMENT
Circular EM/maglev launch to multi-km/s has been directly studied at the conceptual-design level, with a specific peer-reviewed reference case (Fiske et al.) matching this architecture's own layout: circular spin-up ring, tangential release into an exit rail. No full-scale demonstrator has been built — this remains a paper design, not flown or bench-tested hardware. Ground-based nuclear or grid power feeding the ring is a stationary electrical-load question, not a propulsion-design question — no restriction applies.
CITED (directly, from Fiske et al. abstract)
Radial acceleration: >10,000 g
Launch speed: >10 km/s
— both reported achievable for both a small (10 kg) and large (1,000 kg) accelerator class
DERIVED (tangential spin-up rate, cited inputs)
a = Δv/Δt
Δv = 9,000 m/s, Δt ≈ 3,600 s (cited)
a ≈ 2.5 m/s² ≈ ~0.25 g
HOW TO READ THESE TWO NUMBERS TOGETHER The honest picture is more interesting than a single number: the forward (tangential) acceleration that actually builds up speed is gentle — a fraction of a g, spread over roughly an hour, gentler than a car merging onto a highway. But the sideways (centripetal) load from simply going in a circle at that speed is the opposite extreme — over 10,000 g, cited directly from the design study rather than derived here. That radial force, not the spin-up itself, is why circular EM launchers were considered impractical before high-current superconductors existed, and it's the real constraint on what this ring can carry.

Why this matters for the rest of the audit

Every later chamber inherits an assumption from here: that the ground stage can plausibly deliver multi-km/s to something durable enough to survive the trip. That "durable enough" is doing real work — a sustained >10,000 g radial load is far beyond what any living passenger, and most conventional electronics, could survive, which places this architecture firmly in "rugged cargo" territory rather than crewed or delicate-payload territory. If the circular-ring assumption were wrong, Chambers 3 through 6 would be answering the wrong question. It holds up at the conceptual-design level — a specific, matching study exists — but "a matching paper design exists" and "someone has built this exact ring" are different claims, and this audit tries not to let the first one borrow credibility from the second.
VERDICT: CITED for the circular-ring concept and its reported g-load/velocity envelope (Fiske et al.). No built demonstrator exists at this scale — a paper design, not flown hardware. Tangential spin-up rate DERIVED at ~0.25 g from the same source's own numbers.
§ 03 · VELOCITY BUDGET
Velocity Budget — The Target Every Other Chamber Serves
CITED
QUESTION
How much release velocity off the ring is actually needed to net a stable orbit after atmospheric drag losses?
EVIDENCE
LEO orbital velocity~7.6–7.8 km/s
Required total release Δv (gun-launch atmospheric-loss literature, used analogously)~9 km/s
Atmospheric clearance time (hypersonic ground-launch class)<100 seconds
Real gun-launch designs (e.g. Jules Verne gun concept)Still carry an onboard booster for final circularization
Source: University of Washington, "Gun Launch to Space" (aa.washington.edu/files/research/ramaccel) — paraphrased: gun-fired projectiles need ~9 km/s total Δv to reach ~7.6 km/s orbital velocity, the difference lost to atmospheric drag; every cited real design at this class still carries an onboard booster stage.
WHY A GUN-LAUNCH SOURCE IS USED FOR A MAGNETIC LAUNCH ARCHITECTURE This ~9 km/s figure comes from gun-launch literature specifically — Chamber 2's circular magnetic ring is a different technology, and no equivalent published Δv-budget figure for maglev-ring launch was found. The number is used here as an analogous estimate, not a directly-sourced one: atmospheric drag loss during ascent depends mainly on exit velocity, angle, and vehicle shape rather than which technology provided the exit velocity, so a similar hypersonic ground-launch trajectory should lose a broadly similar fraction to drag regardless of whether a gas gun or a magnetic ring provided the exit speed. That's a reasonable physical argument, not a citation — flagged here rather than left silent.
IN PLAIN TERMS"Δv" (delta-v) just means total velocity change needed — think of it as the launch's fuel budget, measured in speed instead of liters. You need more than orbital velocity because you lose some of it fighting through the atmosphere on the way up, the same way a thrown ball loses speed to air resistance. That gap between "what orbit requires" and "what you actually have to achieve at release" is why every real gun-launch design in the literature still needs a small onboard engine at the end — and it's the same reason this architecture carries Chamber 5's ion stage, even though its own launch mechanism isn't a gun. This chamber exists so nothing downstream is measured against a wrong or invented number, and so a reader can trust the ~9 km/s figure enough to build the rest of their own judgment on it.

Where the ~9 km/s actually goes

Roughly 7.6–7.8 km/s of it is genuine orbital velocity — the speed needed to keep falling around the Earth rather than into it. The remaining ~1.2–1.4 km/s is drag loss: friction and shock heating during the brief but violent seconds of climbing through the dense lower atmosphere at hypersonic speed. It's a small fraction of the total budget, but it's exactly the part Chamber 4 is built to reduce, and it's the reason atmospheric transit gets its own chamber instead of being folded into the launch mechanism.
VERDICT: CITED. ~9 km/s is the target. Every subsequent chamber (drag mitigation, ion correction) is scoped against this number.
§ 04 · ATMOSPHERIC TRANSIT
Atmospheric Transit & Trailing-Body Drag Reduction
MIXED
QUESTION
Can a leading body reduce drag/heat load on a trailing payload during hypersonic atmospheric transit?
EVIDENCE
Trailing-vehicle drag/heat, 2D 5-vehicle array, Mach 10<30% of lead vehicle
Trailing-vehicle pressure drag, 3D array, unpowered~20% of lead vehicle
Self-consuming sacrificial torus geometry specificallyNot found in literature
Source: "Drag and Heat Transfer Effects on Hypersonic Vehicles in Close-Proximity Flight," Journal of Aircraft, AIAA, doi:10.2514/1.C035659 — computational study, linear vehicle arrays at Mach 10.
IN PLAIN TERMSThink of drafting behind a truck on the highway, but at ten times the speed of sound: a body traveling just behind another gets a "shadow" in the shock wave and airflow the lead body carves out, and experiences noticeably less drag and heat as a result. That drafting effect is real and has been computer-modeled — a following vehicle can see 70–80% less load than the one in front. What hasn't been tested anywhere found is the specific idea of a sacrificial, ring-shaped leading body that burns away entirely before the real payload catches up to it. That's a reasonable idea built on real physics, but it's still an idea, not a finding.
ASSESSMENT
The general phenomenon — a trailing hypersonic body experiencing substantially reduced drag/heat in formation — is real and quantified (roughly 20–30% of lead-vehicle load). The specific mechanism discussed — a torus-shaped body that fully ablates before the trailing payload reaches it — has no located precedent. The cited study's ~70–80% reduction figure is a reasonable order-of-magnitude anchor for what a torus approach might achieve, but applying it to a self-consuming ring specifically is extrapolation beyond what was tested.

What would actually close this gap

The missing piece isn't the aerodynamics — it's the ablation dynamics: does a torus of a given material shed mass and shape predictably enough, over the ~100-second window from Chamber 3, to keep shielding the payload the whole way up rather than burning through unevenly or breaking apart? That's a materials-and-thermal question, not a fluid-dynamics one, and it's the kind of thing that's normally answered by wind-tunnel or arc-jet testing rather than a literature search. Until that testing exists, this stays MIXED rather than CITED or OPEN — real physics underneath, unverified specific design on top.
VERDICT: General phenomenon CITED. Specific torus implementation OPEN — a design extrapolation from cited data, not itself demonstrated.
§ 05 · ION CORRECTION
Onboard Ion-Propulsion Correction Stage

EVIDENCE

Sources: NASA/UM PEPL NEXT LM4 spec sheet · NASA "Ion Thruster Sets World Record" · Busek Hall Thrusters product page · NASA Psyche mission (Hall-effect thruster, Wikipedia) · NASA LeRC 1991 xenon propulsion study
IN PLAIN TERMS"Isp" (specific impulse) is a fuel-efficiency number — roughly, how many seconds a kilogram of propellant can keep producing a kilogram-force of thrust. Chemical rockets sit around 300–450s; the ion thrusters cited here sit at 2,000–4,000s, meaning they get 5–10× more push per kilogram of fuel. The tradeoff is thrust itself is tiny — hundreds of millinewtons, not tons — so ion propulsion is patient rather than powerful: it takes hours or days of continuous firing to do what a chemical engine does in minutes. That's exactly the right tool for a small trajectory correction after the violent part of the trip is already over, and it's why this is the one chamber with no open questions — this hardware has already flown.

Why this chamber carries the whole architecture's credibility

Chamber 4 is honest about a real, unresolved gap. This chamber is where the architecture gets its actual closure: real, flying hardware, cited performance envelopes, and a worked example that lands well within the propellant-mass budget a small payload could plausibly carry. The trade this chamber makes explicit is patience for propellant: ~5 kg of xenon closes the gap, but only across roughly a week of continuous, gentle thrust rather than the seconds-long violence of Chambers 2 and 4. That's a fair trade for a payload that isn't in a hurry, and a real constraint for one that is. If someone reads only one chamber to decide whether "the last mile" of this idea is credible, this is the one that earns a CITED tag without qualification.
VERDICT: CITED. Real, flying hardware. This is the stage that actually closes the gap left open by Chamber 4 — over a timescale of days, not seconds.
§ 06 · EXPLICITLY EXCLUDED
Explicitly Excluded
BOUNDARY
QUESTION
What is deliberately not designed here, and why is that different from an OPEN chamber?
IN PLAIN TERMSEverything above this chamber is about getting an object to orbit gently and land it in a stable, known trajectory — that's satellite-launch physics, and circular EM/maglev launch concepts like this one have been studied openly in the peer-reviewed literature since at least the 2000s. The two things below are excluded not because the answer is unknown, but because the same real-time steering math that would guide a payload precisely to a tether or a target is the same math used for precision terminal guidance more broadly, regardless of the stated purpose. That's a distinction worth being direct about rather than glossing over.
TERMINAL RENDEZVOUS / PRECISION CORRECTION Sensing position/velocity relative to a target and computing real-time corrections. Unlike Chamber 4 (open because no data exists), this is excluded because the physics is well documented and not secret — real-time rendezvous guidance is used on the ISS, on Dawn, on any orbital docking mission. This auditor has chosen not to work through design specifics for this element regardless of framing or stated target, because the same control-loop capability that guides a payload to a tether is functionally identical to guidance for a precision kinetic strike. This is a fixed decision, not a gap a third party's research would close.
ONBOARD NUCLEAR PROPULSION Fission or fusion pulse propulsion (e.g., Orion-class). Not used anywhere in the architecture above — Chambers 2, 3, and 5 close the velocity budget using EM launch plus ion correction alone. Named here only because it was raised as a theoretical alternative. Same reasoning as above: real, solvable physics, not designed here by choice.
VERDICT: BOUNDARY. Both items are real and solvable by the field generally. Neither is part of the sealed architecture, and neither will be designed here regardless of restated purpose.
§ 07 · READER QUESTIONS

A Few Questions This Audit Expects

Why does an OPEN or MIXED tag matter if the overall idea might still work?
Because "might still work" is only useful if you know exactly which parts are load-bearing and which parts are hopeful extrapolation. Chamber 4's drag reduction is a good example: the general physics is solid, but the specific torus design hasn't been tested by anyone, anywhere. Building on that assumption without flagging it would quietly convert a hope into a fact — which is the one thing this document format is built to prevent.
Is any part of this actually new physics or a breakthrough?
No, and it isn't trying to be. Every cited chamber rests on hardware or phenomena that already exist — circular EM/maglev launch design studies, hoop-stress mechanics, hypersonic formation drafting, flight-proven ion thrusters. The interesting part of this architecture is the combination and sequencing, not any single component.
