SYS-01 · HABITAT STRUCTURE AND ROTATION

The Torus · Gravity by Geometry

The rotating habitat is the primary structural node. Everything else is field systems layered around it. The torus must sustain 0.5g at the crew floor continuously for 900 days — that is the non-negotiable biological constraint the structure serves.

Crew complement: 8. The vessel is sized for 8. A crew of 6 would operate with surplus consumables and hab volume — a meaningful margin. A crew of 9 would exceed the consumables budget and hab volume per crew without redesign. 8 is the design point. 6 is the minimum viable crew for redundancy in critical systems operation.

MTBF target: >50,000 hours. Backup: emergency slow-stop protocol over 48 hours preserves crew vestibular integrity.

FAILURE MODE · F-02 REVISED · GEMINI NODE

Previous v3.1-ENG.1 mitigation was brittle: single-coil binary failsafe — quench triggers dump to resistive load. If dump resistor fails simultaneously, field collapses to zero and boom is destroyed. Binary failure of a non-replaceable system at 150m standoff is not acceptable.

Revised mitigation — dual-interlaced dipole: If Coil A quenches, mutual inductance coupling transfers inductive load to Coil B, stepping total field from 2T to ~1.1T over 90 seconds. Controlled gradient descent, not collapse. GCR deflection at 1.1T covers ~70% of target spectrum. Degraded but operational. Coil A recools and recharges while Coil B holds partial deflection. Full restoration: 4–6 hours. Crew exposure during degraded window: ~0.4 mSv — significantly improved from 1.2 mSv in single-coil scenario. Boom structure preserved throughout.

ENGINEERING NOTE · THE TOPOLOGY SOLVE v2.0 failed because one magnetic system was asked to do both jobs. To cover both functions, the system required field strengths and geometries that exceeded total mass budget by 340%. Separate the functions, size each system for its single job: System A oversized for biology, undersized for habitat. System B is the reverse. Neither compromise applies when they are independent. This is why the mass budget closed.

FAILURE MODE · F-04 · H₂S SPIKE REVISED · GEMINI NODE

Previous v3.1-ENG.1 mitigation: Any sensor reading above 83 ppm triggers "immediate H₂S flush and revival protocol initiation." Critical flaw: rapid H₂S flush while core temperature remains at 32°C induces massive reperfusion shock. Biological vessel violently jolted into waking state with mitotic machinery mid-suppression — exactly the damage the biostasis protocol is designed to prevent.

Revised emergency protocol — sequential staged revival:

Stage 1 (0–5 min): H₂S delivery halted. H₂S antagonist (hydroxocobalamin, 5g IV) administered via automated infusion. This neutralizes circulating H₂S without triggering full metabolic reversal. Core temperature maintained at 32°C.

Stage 2 (5–30 min): Controlled thermal rise initiated at +0.5°C per 5 minutes. Target: 34°C. Slow enough that mitotic machinery is not shocked into sudden activity. H₂S levels monitored — if below 40 ppm, full flush completed. If still elevated, second antagonist dose at 15 min.

Stage 3 (30–90 min): Core temperature raised to 36°C at same controlled rate. DADLE reversal agent (naltrindole, titrated) introduced at 34°C core — metabolic rate rises gradually. Fields verified hot throughout all stages.

Stage 4 (90 min onward): Standard revival protocol completion. Consciousness returns into confirmed field geometry. Full medical assessment before any crew member stands.

The emergency protocol is a torus flow, not a hard stop. Mitotic fidelity is preserved throughout the staged recovery. Mass cost: hydroxocobalamin and naltrindole emergency reserves added to consumables budget — approximately 2 kg additional.

ENGINEERING NOTE · THE REVIVAL WINDOW The most vulnerable point in the 900-day mission is the revival window after 45 days of metabolic suppression. Mitotic fidelity is lowest here. If field systems are offline during revival, the crew restarts in a geometry their biology does not recognize. The 8 minutes of pre-revival field verification is the mission's most critical engineering checkpoint. Both the normal and emergency revival protocols maintain fields hot throughout.

SYS-08 · MASS BUDGET · LEO ASSEMBLY

4,800 Tonnes · Where It Goes · Zero Margin

ZERO MARGIN — EXPLICIT RISK STATEMENT This budget closes at zero contingency mass. In engineering practice, zero margin means any unanticipated mass increase — even a 0.5% cable harness weight overrun — breaks the budget and requires reduction elsewhere. This is not unusual for proposed deep space architectures, but it means manufacturing and design precision requirements are unprecedented. Zero margin is the single greatest schedule and cost risk in the Solitaire program. Every design decision that adds mass must simultaneously identify where that mass is recovered. This constraint is non-negotiable if E=1 holds on the vehicle mass budget. It is named here, not softened.

FAILURE MODE · F-05 · ENGINE FAILURE CAUTION

Single NTR engine failure during burn. Consequence: reduced total thrust, extended burn duration, potential trajectory deviation. Mitigation: 3-engine cluster allows mission completion on 2 engines for all planned burns. Extended burn time on 2 engines remains within propellant budget. Trajectory correction burns available from attitude control system. Crew not required to perform EVA on a failed nuclear engine — no maintenance protocol exists for in-space NTR repair. A failed engine stays failed.
